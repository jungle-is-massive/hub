Who gets what, how, and how often.

The principle we keep coming back to

Tier sets the format and the intimacy — not the quality of the news. A Tier 1 contact doesn't get "better" stories; they get the lightest, fastest, most personal version of them.

The most senior people are the busiest. Victoria at AAR would rather have two bullets on WhatsApp than the glossiest case study in her inbox. Across every group — intermediaries, prospects, clients, press — the winning move is the same: give them the TLDR, in the channel they actually read, and don't bombard them.

Rows are the kinds of news we generate; columns are the three tiers. Each cell is the proposed default vehicle and timing — the thing that flips to "Sent" on the board. Treat every cell as a question, not an answer.

Four groups, three tiers. Groups are who they are; tiers are how close the relationship is and therefore how we reach them. A contact's group gives a default tier, but you can pull anyone up or down.

Group → default tier (starting point): Intermediaries span all three tiers — your gold relationships sit in Tier 1, the rest in Tier 2. Warm prospects → Tier 2. Clients → Tier 2 (they already know us; this is nurture). Press / media & campaign contacts → Tier 3, newsletter-only until a relationship warms up.

The kinds of news worth sharing — and where the content for each already lives, so nothing here is extra work, just rounding up what exists.

Two axes: the vehicle (how it travels) and the format (how it's dressed). The same client win can be a one-line WhatsApp to one person and a designed newsletter block to a list.

How often each vehicle fires, and how we keep the backlog full enough to feed it without scrambling.
